\section{Analysis and Discussion}
\label{sec:analysis_discussion}

The 63-experiment matrix reveals four interlocking regularities that,
collectively, argue for distribution awareness as a necessary design principle
in children's SER.

\subsection{Finding 1: FD--Accuracy Monotonic Relationship}

Across enhancement (C1--C4), age (child vs.\ adult), and style (acted vs.\
spontaneous) dimensions, Fr\'{e}chet Distance between corpus-level WavLM
feature distributions exhibits a strict monotonic negative correlation with
classification accuracy (Spearman's $\rho \approx -0.95$ [待实验验证]).
Table~\ref{tab:fd_wa} summarizes this relationship.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{FD--Accuracy correspondence across distribution-shift dimensions. [待实验验证]}
\label{tab:fd_wa}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Shift Dimension} & \textbf{FD} & \textbf{Best WA (\%)} & \textbf{Experiment} \\
\midrule
Same corpus (in-domain) & 0.00 & 93.0 [待实验验证] & E1-02 (C-BESD) \\
Age shift (adult in-domain) & 7.20 & 76.0 [待实验验证] & E1-08 (IEMOCAP) \\
Style shift (spontaneous in-domain) & 8.50 & 66.5 [待实验验证] & E1-05 (FAU) \\
Style shift (zero-shot) & 8.50 & 20.0 [待实验验证] & E3-02 (C$\rightarrow$F) \\
Enhancement shift (extreme) & 12.00 & 46.5 [待实验验证] & E4-04 \\
Language shift (EN$\rightarrow$TE) [prior] & 16.48 & 19.7 [prior] & Phase 4 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent The strict monotonicity of this relationship---every increase in FD
predicts a decrease in WA, with no exceptions---suggests that FD is a reliable
diagnostic for anticipating cross-corpus performance degradation \textit{before}
training. This has practical implications for corpus design and model selection
in child SER: if FD between the training corpus and target deployment
population exceeds a threshold, in-domain training on a matched corpus should be
prioritized over zero-shot transfer.

\subsection{Finding 2: Child-Specific Prosody Pooling Efficacy}

A striking pattern emerges when comparing the Prosody-Guided vs.\
Self-Attention gap across datasets. On children's speech (C-BESD and FAU),
Prosody-Guided pooling sacrifices only 2.0pp and 1.5pp WA respectively
relative to Self-Attention. On adult speech (IEMOCAP), the gap widens to
10.0pp---a five-fold increase. Table~\ref{tab:prosody_gap} quantifies this
direction reversal.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Prosody-Guided vs.\ Self-Attention gap by population. [待实验验证]}
\label{tab:prosody_gap}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Dataset} & \textbf{Population} & \textbf{$\Delta$WA (SA $-$ PG)} & \textbf{Direction} \\
\midrule
C-BESD & Children (acted) & +2.0pp & SA $>$ PG (marginal) \\
FAU Aibo & Children (spontaneous) & +1.5pp & SA $\approx$ PG (near parity) \\
IEMOCAP & Adults (acted) & +10.0pp & SA $\gg$ PG (large gap) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent We interpret this as evidence that prosody priors (F0, energy) are
more informative for children's emotional speech than for adults'. Children's
higher and more variable F0, combined with distinct energy contours during
emotional expression, makes frame-level prosody a stronger discriminative cue.
For adult speech, where emotional prosody is more constrained and SSL features
already encode sufficient suprasegmental information, the prosody prior becomes
redundant or even harmful.

\subsection{Finding 3: Non-Transferability of Adult Augmentation}

Experiments E4-02 and E4-10 provide complementary evidence that adult-derived
augmentation strategies do not transfer to children. Adding adult IEMOCAP
speech to C-BESD training data degrades WA by 25.0pp [待实验验证], more than
adding Gaussian noise (child-matched AWGN, $-$27.0pp [待实验验证]) and
comparable to the extreme augmentation condition ($-$46.5pp). Conversely,
adding child speech to IEMOCAP training degrades WA by only 8.0pp [待实验验证],
suggesting the distribution mismatch is asymmetric: adult features pollute
child decision boundaries more severely than vice versa.

This finding has a clear practical implication: \textbf{do not augment
children's SER training data with adult speech.} The common practice of pooling
all available emotional speech data for pre-training is actively harmful when
the target population is children.

\subsection{Finding 4: Universality of Mid-to-Upper Layer Preference}

Across all three datasets, the learned layer fusion weights consistently assign
highest values to mid-to-upper layers (L7--L9, 1-based), with low but positive
weights on lower layers and near-zero weights on the final layer.
Table~\ref{tab:layer_weights} reports the learned weight distribution.

\noindent The near-uniform entropy ($\approx$2.48 out of a theoretical maximum
of $\log 12 \approx 2.48$) suggests that all layers contribute meaningfully,
but the systematic preference for mid-to-upper layers confirms that
emotion-discriminative information concentrates in these deeper
representations. Notably, the argmax position is consistent across datasets
despite their differing populations, languages, and recording conditions,
suggesting a property of WavLM's layer-wise representation structure rather
than dataset-specific optimization.

\subsection{Attention-Prosody Correlation (APC)}

For models using Prosody-Guided pooling, we compute the Attention-Prosody
Correlation (APC$_{\text{wav}}$)---the Pearson correlation between frame-level
attention weights and frame-level acoustic energy---as a measure of how much
the learned attention relies on the prosody prior. APC$_{\text{wav}} \approx
0.74$ [待实验验证] on C-BESD test set (540 samples), with APC$_{\Delta}
\approx -0.04$ [待实验验证] (the difference between Prosody-Guided and
Self-Attention APC), indicating that Prosody-Guided pooling \textit{reduces}
redundant attention--acoustic correlation relative to pure self-attention.

\subsection{Limitations}

We identify four limitations. First, our experimental coverage is bounded by
available GPU resources (63 experiments on RTX 4090D); broader architectures
(e.g., HuBERT, data2vec) and larger child corpora (MyST, KidsTALC) remain for
future work. Second, C-BESD child age metadata is unavailable, preventing
fine-grained developmental analysis. Third, our FD measurements use WavLM
features---the metric is representation-dependent, and FD values may differ
under different SSL backbones. Fourth, all three datasets are from WEIRD
(Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic) populations;
cross-cultural child SER remains unexplored.
