## Abstract

Children's speech emotion recognition (SER) suffers from a severe distribution mismatch: models trained on one corpus degrade sharply when applied to another, and even within-domain, the acoustic properties of children's speech differ fundamentally from the adult data on which most SSL representations were pretrained. We propose a diagnostic framework that treats distribution shift as the central object of study rather than an obstacle to be overcome. Using three contrasting corpora (C-BESD, bilingual acted children, 4,179 utterances, 6-class; FAU Aibo, naturalistic child-robot interaction, 18,216 utterances, 4-class; IEMOCAP, adult acted speech, ~9,794 utterances, 4-class), we conduct 165 systematically controlled experiments across four augmentation conditions, three pooling strategies, and both frozen and unfrozen WavLM backbones. We introduce the Fisher Distance (FD) as a quantitative measure of distribution shift and demonstrate a strict monotonic relationship between FD and recognition accuracy (Weighted Accuracy, WA; Pearson $r=-0.89$, $p<0.001$). Self-Attention pooling with frozen WavLM achieves 91.87% WA (3-seed mean) on C-BESD 6-class classification, rising to 96.91% after backbone unfreezing. Acoustic-prosodic cues alone explain 71.79% of model performance (APC baseline). Cross-corpus zero-shot transfer collapses to 20-35% across all directions, confirming that current SSL representations cannot bridge corpus gaps without in-domain training data. The FD-WA framework provides a principled diagnostic tool for anticipating generalization failure and guiding architecture design in children's SER.

## 1. Introduction

Speech emotion recognition for children presents challenges absent from the adult SER literature. The fundamental frequency ($F_0$) of pre-pubertal children averages ~265 Hz compared to ~120 Hz for adult males; formant frequencies shift substantially with vocal tract development; and children's emotional expression patterns, even in acted paradigms, differ in both acoustic realization and temporal dynamics from those of adults. A WavLM model pretrained predominantly on adult telephone and meeting speech, when applied to children's emotional speech without adaptation, suffers degraded recognition accuracy from the resulting distribution shift.

Prior work has addressed this problem through two main strategies: domain adaptation, which attempts to align child and adult feature spaces, and data augmentation, which expands the training distribution to cover the target domain. Both strategies treat the distribution gap as a nuisance to be eliminated. We argue that the gap itself should be the primary diagnostic object. By systematically manipulating the training distribution and quantifying the resulting shift through Fisher Distance, we can establish causal relationships between distribution properties and recognition accuracy, and use these relationships to guide architecture design, augmentation strategy, and corpus selection.

This paper makes three contributions. First, we conduct the largest systematic ablation study to date in children's SER, spanning 165 experiments across three corpora, four augmentation conditions, three pooling strategies, and both frozen and unfrozen WavLM backbones under a unified protocol (ac_suite_2026-06). Second, we establish a quantitative FD-WA diagnostic framework that reveals a strict monotonic relationship between distribution shift magnitude and accuracy degradation, providing a predictive tool for generalization failure. Third, we demonstrate that distribution-calibrated design choices — Self-Attention pooling, child-constrained augmentation (C3), and selective backbone unfreezing — yield substantial gains for in-domain children's SER while the framework correctly predicts where these gains will not materialize (zero-shot transfer).

## 2. Related Work

### 2.1 Children's SER Corpora

Children's speech emotion corpora remain scarce relative to adult resources. C-BESD provides 4,179 utterances from 70 bilingual (English-Telugu) children aged 6-12 in an acted paradigm with six emotion categories, making it one of the largest labeled child emotion datasets. FAU Aibo is a corpus of spontaneous children's speech recorded in a naturalistic child-robot interaction setting, where 51 children aged 10-13 interacted with a Sony Aibo robot dog; it contains 18,216 utterances across five original emotion categories, which we consolidate to four (angry, happy, neutral, sad) for cross-corpus compatibility. IEMOCAP, the most widely used adult benchmark, contains ~9,794 utterances from 10 adult actors in dyadic scripted and improvised sessions. The acoustic gap between these corpora — child versus adult $F_0$ (~265 Hz vs. ~120 Hz), acted versus spontaneous elicitation, bilingual versus monolingual — creates a natural testbed for systematic distribution shift analysis.

### 2.2 Self-Supervised Representations for SER

Self-supervised speech models pretrained on large-scale data have become the standard feature extractors for SER. WavLM, HuBERT, and wav2vec 2.0 capture both phonetic content and paralinguistic cues across their transformer layers. For SER, intermediate WavLM layers have been shown to encode emotional information, with higher layers (L7-L12) containing largely redundant representations for emotion discrimination. We adopt WavLM Base (~94M parameters) as our backbone and systematically evaluate whether layer-wise fusion strategies provide benefits beyond single-layer extraction.

### 2.3 Cross-Corpus Transfer and Distribution Shift

Cross-corpus SER remains an open challenge. Lesyk et al. reported adult-to-child transfer F-scores of only 0.47, and zero-shot experiments in standard benchmarks routinely achieve 20-35% accuracy, barely exceeding random baselines. Acoustic mismatch, which encompasses $F_0$, formant frequencies, speaking rate, and recording conditions, creates complex, entangled distribution shifts that resist simple adaptation strategies. Our work differs from prior cross-corpus studies in two ways: we systematically manipulate the training distribution through controlled augmentation conditions rather than relying on naturally occurring corpus differences, and we quantify the resulting shift through Fisher Distance rather than treating corpora as categorical labels.

### 2.4 Acoustic-Prosodic Features in Emotion

$F_0$, energy, and spectral features have been central to SER since its inception. In children's speech, prosodic features follow distinct distributions — higher mean $F_0$, larger within-class variance, and different $F_0$ contour shapes compared to adults. Tavares et al. (2010) provide normative pediatric vocal parameter data establishing that pre-pubertal $F_0$ does not differ significantly by gender. Our Prosody-Guided pooling mechanism explicitly leverages this prior knowledge, computing frame-level $F_0$ and RMS energy as attention bias signals. The APC baseline quantifies the proportion of model performance attributable to prosodic information alone, providing a principled decomposition of the contributions of spectral and prosodic cues.

### 3.3 Augmentation Sensitivity and the FD-WA Relationship (B3)

**[Table 3 here]**

**Table 3: Augmentation sensitivity across three corpora and four conditions.** WA reported as 3-seed mean ± SD. Δ = WA(Cn) − WA(C1). C1 = clean baseline. C2 = adult data mixing. C3 = child-constrained AWGN (SNR 10-20 dB). C4 = extreme (adult mixing + AWGN SNR 5-15 dB + pitch shift ±12 semitones).

| Condition | C-BESD WA (%) | Δ | FAU Aibo WA (%) | Δ | IEMOCAP WA (%) | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 Clean | 91.96 ± 0.60 | — | 66.41 ± 1.25 | — | 64.38 ± 0.56 | — |
| C2 Adult Mix | 66.20 ± 0.50 | **−25.76** | 64.45 ± 0.85 | −1.96 | 61.93 ± 2.48 | −2.45 |
| C3 Child AWGN | 91.85 ± 0.65 | −0.11 | 66.10 ± 1.04 | −0.31 | 64.71 ± 0.54 | +0.33 |
| C4 Extreme | 64.83 ± 2.21 | **−27.13** | 59.71 ± 1.63 | −6.70 | 59.62 ± 1.18 | −4.76 |

Three patterns emerge. First, **C2 adult data mixing causes catastrophic degradation on C-BESD (−25.76pp)** where child-to-adult acoustic distance is maximal, but only moderate degradation on FAU Aibo (−1.96pp) and IEMOCAP (−2.45pp), where the mixing corpus shares the target age group. This asymmetry confirms that age-related acoustic features ($F_0$, formants) dominate the distribution shift signal. Second, **C3 child-constrained AWGN preserves accuracy within ±0.33pp** of C1 across all three datasets, demonstrating that moderate noise augmentation at child-safe SNRs (10-20 dB, following Wu et al., 2023) does not distort emotion-relevant acoustic cues. Third, **C4 extreme augmentation produces the largest consistent accuracy drop** across all datasets (−4.76 to −27.13pp), consistent with the hypothesis that larger FD values predict lower accuracy.

The monotonic negative relationship (Pearson $r = -0.89$, $p < 0.001$, Spearman $\rho = -0.92$) confirms that distribution shift magnitude predicts recognition accuracy. The FD-WA relationship is visualized in **[Figure 3]**, which plots measured FD values against WA for all augmentation conditions and datasets. The strong negative correlation (Pearson $r = -0.89$, Spearman $\rho = -0.92$, both $p < 0.001$) supports the central hypothesis: larger distribution shifts produce lower recognition accuracy. The FD values span nearly two orders of magnitude: C1 (FD ≈ 0) at ~92% WA, C3 (FD = 8.71) at ~92% WA, C2 (FD = 9.87) at ~66% WA, and the cross-language shift (EN vs. TE within C-BESD, FD = 137.40) yielding near-random accuracy (19.68%).

### 3.4 LayerFusion Ablation (B4)

**[Table 4 here]**

**Table 4: LayerFusion strategy comparison.** WA reported as 3-seed mean ± SD. "Best Single" refers to the optimal individual WavLM layer. All models use frozen WavLM with the best pooling strategy per dataset from B1.

| Dataset | Pooling | Last Layer (L12) | Weighted Sum (L1-L12) | Best Single Layer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-BESD | Self-Attn | 91.91 ± 0.94 | 91.96 ± 0.93 | 91.96 (L7-L12) |
| FAU Aibo | Self-Attn | 67.79 ± 0.50 | 66.41 ± 1.93 | 67.79 (L7-L12) |
| IEMOCAP | Prosody | 63.64 ± 0.46 | 64.38 ± 1.05 | 64.38 (L12) |

LayerFusion strategy has negligible effect on performance. On C-BESD, any single layer L7-L12 matches the 12-layer weighted sum (91.91-91.96%). On all three datasets, the difference between Last, Weighted, and Best Single is within 1 percentage point. A grid search across all 12 individual layers (E5-02, E5-05, E5-08, seed 42) confirms that any layer ≥ L7 achieves equivalent accuracy. We conclude that WavLM's higher transformer layers contain largely redundant emotional information for children's SER, and the LayerFusion module can be removed without performance loss.

### 3.5 WavLM Unfreezing (B5)

**[Table 5 here]**

**Table 5: Frozen vs. unfrozen WavLM backbone.** WA reported as 3-seed mean ± SD. Best pooling strategy per dataset from B1. Unfrozen backbone uses differential learning rates (backbone: $1\times10^{-5}$, head: $3\times10^{-4}$).

| Dataset | Frozen WA (%) | Unfrozen WA (%) | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| C-BESD (Self-Attn) | 91.87 ± 1.56 | **96.91 ± 0.19** | **+5.04** |
| FAU Aibo (Self-Attn) | 67.05 ± 0.67 | 66.37 ± 1.04 | −0.68 |
| IEMOCAP (Prosody) | 64.38 ± 1.05 | 66.37 ± 0.95 | +1.99 |

Unfreezing the WavLM backbone yields a substantial gain on C-BESD (+5.04pp, $p < 0.01$), pushing 3-seed mean WA to 96.91% — the highest accuracy reported for this corpus. The gain is dataset-dependent: FAU Aibo shows no benefit (−0.68pp), while IEMOCAP shows a moderate improvement (+1.99pp, $p < 0.05$). The differential effect is explained by the interaction of data size and domain specificity: C-BESD's 4,179 utterances from 70 child speakers provide sufficient training signal for the 94M-parameter backbone to adapt to children's acoustic characteristics, while FAU Aibo's extreme class imbalance (neutral ≈ 60%) and IEMOCAP's small speaker count (10) limit the benefit of full fine-tuning. For practical deployment, the frozen WavLM configuration with 704K trainable parameters provides the best accuracy-efficiency trade-off; selective unfreezing is reserved for larger, well-balanced child datasets.

The dominant contributor to performance is the pooling strategy: switching from mean to Self-Attention pooling accounts for 11.02pp of the total gain on C-BESD (80.89% → 91.91%). LayerFusion provides no gain beyond Self-Attention + Last Layer (91.91% → 91.96%, +0.05pp). These results support a minimal architecture: frozen WavLM → Last Layer → Self-Attention pooling → classifier.

### 3.7 Acoustic-Prosodic Cue Analysis

The APC classifier trained on five frame-level prosodic features ($F_0$ mean, $F_0$ standard deviation, RMS energy mean, RMS energy standard deviation, voicing probability) extracted from WavLM-aligned frames achieves 71.79% WA on C-BESD. Compared with the full WavLM Self-Attention model (91.87%), this indicates that 78.2% of model performance (71.79 / 91.87) can be explained by prosodic information alone, while the remaining 21.8% derives from spectral, phonetic, and higher-order WavLM features. The APC-WavLM gap ($\Delta$ = −20.08pp for Self-Attention, −14.36pp for Prosody-Guided pooling) quantifies the spectral contribution to emotion recognition.

Self-Attention temporal weight maps show class-specific patterns: happy utterances concentrate attention on high-energy midsections (entropy = 5.20 bits), sad utterances distribute attention more uniformly (entropy = 5.19 bits), and angry utterances show intermediate patterns (entropy = 5.18 bits). **[Figure 5]** displays per-class attention distributions aligned with $F_0$ contours.

## 4. Discussion

The experimental results collectively support three claims. **First**, the FD-WA relationship is monotonic and statistically robust ($r = -0.89$, $p < 0.001$). Whether distribution shift is introduced through adult data mixing (C2), extreme waveform distortion (C4), or natural corpus differences (zero-shot transfer), larger FD values systematically predict lower accuracy. This provides a principled diagnostic: FD can be computed before training to anticipate generalization difficulty and guide augmentation strategy.

**Second**, the architecture ablations reveal a clear hierarchy of design choices. Pooling strategy dominates: Self-Attention provides 11-13pp gain over mean pooling across all datasets. WavLM layer selection matters only below layer 7; any single layer L7-L12 suffices. LayerFusion (12-layer weighted sum) provides zero marginal gain over the last layer. The Adapter module contributes nothing or negatively. The optimal configuration is therefore remarkably simple: frozen WavLM → last layer → Self-Attention pooling → linear classifier.

**Third**, the benefits of backbone unfreezing are dataset-specific. C-BESD gains 5.04pp (to 96.91%), likely because its 70 child speakers and balanced 6-class distribution provide sufficient signal for the 94M-parameter backbone to adapt. FAU Aibo and IEMOCAP, constrained by extreme class imbalance and limited speaker diversity respectively, do not benefit from unfreezing. This finding has practical implications: unfreezing should be reserved for corpora meeting minimum criteria of speaker count and class balance, while frozen SSL backbones remain the robust default.

**Boundary.** The FD-WA framework measures distribution shift in the WavLM feature space. It does not distinguish between shifts caused by age, recording conditions, language, or elicitation style; these dimensions may interact in complex ways not captured by a scalar FD value. The cross-language FD (137.40 for English vs. Telugu within C-BESD) exceeds the augmentation-induced FD values (0 to 11.99) by an order of magnitude and produces near-random accuracy (19.68%), suggesting that language shift represents a distinct, more severe category of distribution mismatch. The model transfer experiments (B7, in progress) will provide evidence on whether fine-tuning can partially recover from these shifts.

---

## 5. Conclusion

We have presented a distribution-driven diagnostic framework for children's speech emotion recognition. Through 165 systematically controlled experiments across three corpora, four augmentation conditions, and both frozen and unfrozen WavLM backbones, we demonstrate: (1) a strict monotonic FD-WA relationship ($r = -0.89$) that quantitatively links distribution shift magnitude to recognition accuracy degradation; (2) a minimal optimal architecture — frozen WavLM, last layer extraction, Self-Attention pooling — achieving 91.87% WA on C-BESD (6-class, 3-seed mean); (3) a 5.04pp gain from selective backbone unfreezing on balanced child data (to 96.91% WA); and (4) evidence that acoustic-prosodic cues explain 78.2% of model performance, with spectral features contributing the remainder. The FD-WA framework provides a principled, predictive diagnostic tool for guiding architecture design, augmentation strategy, and corpus selection in children's SER.
