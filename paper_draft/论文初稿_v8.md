# Distribution-Driven Diagnostic Framework for Children's Speech Emotion Recognition

**Authors**: [Author names withheld for review]

## Abstract

Children's speech emotion recognition (SER) suffers from a fundamental distribution mismatch: models trained on adult speech degrade severely when applied to children, and even within child speech, cross-corpus generalization remains poor. We propose a diagnostic framework that treats distribution shift as the primary object of study rather than an obstacle to overcome. Using three contrasting corpora — C-BESD (bilingual children, 4,179 utterances), FAU Aibo (naturalistic child-robot interaction, 18,216 utterances), and IEMOCAP (adult acted speech, ~9,794 utterances) — we systematically manipulate the training distribution along four controlled augmentation conditions and measure the resulting Fisher Distance (FD) between feature distributions. Our results demonstrate a strict monotonic relationship between FD and recognition accuracy (Weighted Accuracy, WA), with language shift producing the largest FD (137.40) and near-random cross-lingual accuracy (19.68%). Within-domain, self-attention pooling with frozen WavLM achieves 92.92% WA on C-BESD, rising to 96.91% after unfreezing the backbone. Acoustic-prosodic cues explain 71.79% of model performance, and t-SNE visualization confirms improved class separability after distribution calibration. We release a complete ablation matrix of 165 experiments across three corpora, four augmentation conditions, and three pooling strategies as a benchmark for distribution-aware children's SER.

**Keywords**: children's speech emotion recognition, distribution shift, Fisher distance, self-supervised representations, cross-corpus generalization

## 1. Introduction

Speech emotion recognition for children presents unique challenges that are absent from the adult SER literature. Children's fundamental frequency ($F_0$) averages ~265 Hz compared to ~120 Hz for adult males, their formant frequencies are substantially higher, and their emotional expression patterns differ qualitatively from those of adults. These acoustic divergences create a severe distribution shift: models trained on adult speech routinely fail when applied to children's speech, and even models trained on one child corpus generalize poorly to another.

Previous work has addressed this problem primarily through domain adaptation and data augmentation, treating the distribution gap as a nuisance to be eliminated. We argue instead that the distribution gap itself should be the central diagnostic object. By systematically manipulating the training distribution and quantifying the resulting shift through Fisher Distance (FD), we can establish causal relationships between distribution properties and recognition accuracy — and use these relationships to guide architecture design, augmentation strategy, and corpus selection.

This paper makes three contributions. First, we conduct a comprehensive ablation study across three corpora (C-BESD, FAU Aibo, IEMOCAP), four controlled augmentation conditions (C1-C4: clean, adult-mixed, child-constrained, extreme), three pooling strategies (mean, self-attention, prosody-guided), and both frozen and unfrozen WavLM backbones, totaling 165 experiments. Second, we establish a quantitative FD-WA diagnostic framework that reveals a strict monotonic relationship between distribution shift magnitude and accuracy degradation. Third, we provide evidence that distribution-calibrated design choices — self-attention pooling, child-constrained augmentation, and selective backbone unfreezing — yield substantial gains for in-domain children's SER while correctly predicting zero-shot generalization failures.

## 2. Related Work

### 2.1 Children's SER Corpora and Benchmarks

Children's speech emotion corpora are scarce compared to adult resources. C-BESD provides 4,179 utterances from 70 bilingual (English-Telugu) children aged 6-12 in an acted paradigm with six emotion categories. FAU Aibo is a German corpus of spontaneous children's speech from 51 children aged 10-13, recorded in a naturalistic child-robot interaction setting where children interacted with a Sony Aibo robot dog; it contains 18,216 utterances across five emotion categories, which we consolidate to four (angry, happy, neutral, sad) for cross-corpus alignment. IEMOCAP, the most widely used adult benchmark, contains ~9,794 utterances from 10 adult actors in dyadic scripted and improvised sessions, providing a natural adult control condition. The acoustic gap between these corpora — child vs. adult $F_0$ of ~265 Hz vs. ~120 Hz, acted vs. spontaneous vs. acted elicitation, bilingual vs. monolingual — creates a natural testbed for distribution shift analysis.

### 2.2 Self-Supervised Representations for SER

Self-supervised learning (SSL) models pretrained on large-scale speech data have become the de facto feature extractors for SER. WavLM, HuBERT, and wav2vec 2.0 capture both phonetic content and paralinguistic cues across their transformer layers. For SER specifically, intermediate WavLM layers (L6-L12) have been shown to contain rich emotional information, with layer-wise fusion providing marginal benefits over single-layer extraction. We adopt WavLM Base as our backbone and systematically examine which layers and fusion strategies are optimal for children's speech, finding that any single layer ≥L7 achieves performance equivalent to learned weighted fusion across all 12 layers (Table 4).

### 2.3 Cross-Corpus Transfer and Distribution Shift

Cross-corpus SER remains an open challenge. Lesyk et al. reported adult-to-child transfer F-scores of only 0.47, and zero-shot experiments routinely show 20-35% absolute accuracy, barely above random. Acoustic mismatch — $F_0$, formant frequencies, speaking rate, microphone conditions — creates complex, entangled distribution shifts that resist simple adaptation strategies. Our work extends this line of research by providing a quantitative diagnostic: the FD-WA framework measures distribution shift magnitude and predicts generalization failure before training.

### 2.4 Acoustic-Prosodic Features in Emotion

$F_0$, energy, and spectral features have been central to SER since its inception. In children's speech, prosodic features follow distinct distributions — higher mean $F_0$, larger within-class variance, and different $F_0$ contour shapes compared to adults. Tavares et al. provide normative pediatric vocal parameter data for 240 children aged 4-12, establishing that pre-pubertal $F_0$ does not differ significantly by gender. Our prosody-guided pooling mechanism explicitly leverages this prior knowledge, computing frame-level $F_0$ and RMS energy as attention bias signals. The acoustic-prosodic cue (APC) baseline quantifies the proportion of model performance attributable to prosodic information alone.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Datasets and Preprocessing

**Table 1: Dataset characteristics and standardization.**

| Property | C-BESD (MY) | FAU Aibo | IEMOCAP |
|----------|-------------|----------|---------|
| Utterances | 4,179 | 18,216 | ~9,794 |
| Speakers | 70 children | 51 children | 10 adults |
| Age range | 6–12 years | 10–13 years | Adult |
| Emotion classes | 6 (ANG, DIS, FEA, HAP, NEU, SAD) | 4 (ANG, HAP, NEU, SAD) | 4 (ANG, HAP, NEU, SAD) |
| Elicitation | Acted, bilingual (EN+TE) | Spontaneous, child-robot | Acted, dyadic |
| Language | English + Telugu | German | English |
| Recording environment | Studio | Classroom/home | Studio |

All audio is resampled to 16kHz mono, peak-normalized, and truncated to a maximum of 4 seconds (200 frames at 50Hz WavLM frame rate). Speaker-independent splits use deterministic MD5-hash assignment with a fixed random seed (42), producing 70%/15%/15% train/validation/test partitions with zero speaker overlap. For zero-shot experiments (E3), C-BESD uses a 4-class subset (discarding disgust and fear) to align label spaces with FAU and IEMOCAP.

### 3.2 Architecture

Our model consists of four components: (1) WavLM Base (~94M parameters) as the frozen SSL backbone, producing 768-dimensional frame-level features at 50Hz; (2) an optional 12-layer fusion module with learnable softmax weights; (3) a pooling layer implementing one of three strategies (mean, multi-head self-attention, or prosody-guided attention); and (4) an SEMLP classifier projecting pooled features to class logits. The total trainable parameter count is approximately 704K when WavLM is frozen.

**Figure 1**: Architecture overview. WavLM Base extracts 768-dim frame features → 12-layer fusion (weighted sum) → Pooling (mean/self-attention/prosody-guided) → SEMLP classifier. Trainable components: ~704K parameters.

### 3.3 Experimental Protocol

All experiments follow protocol `ac_suite_2026-06`. Training uses online WavLM feature extraction (no pre-stored features), batch size 16, AdamW optimizer with learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, weight decay $1\times10^{-3}$, label smoothing 0.1, and early stopping with patience 15 epochs (maximum 100 epochs). Each experiment is repeated with three model initialization seeds (42, 123, 456) while the data split seed is fixed at 42 to ensure identical train/val/test partitions. FAU experiments use a stronger regularization profile (weight decay $5\times10^{-3}$, label smoothing 0.15, pooling dropout 0.3, gradient clipping 1.0) due to the larger sample size and naturalistic recording variability.

### 3.4 Augmentation Conditions (B3)

We define four controlled conditions that systematically manipulate the training distribution along two mechanisms: data mixing (Mechanism A: adding out-of-distribution samples) and waveform distortion (Mechanism B: acoustic transformations preserving emotion labels).

- **C1 (Clean baseline)**: Original data, no processing. Expected FD ≈ 0.
- **C2 (Adult data mixing, pure Mechanism A)**: Child training set mixed with IEMOCAP adult samples at approximately 1:0.5 ratio within matching emotion classes. Expected FD ↑ moderate. For IEMOCAP (adult data), C2 mixes in FAU child spontaneous speech instead, creating a cross-age + cross-style dual shift.
- **C3 (Child-constrained augmentation, pure Mechanism B)**: Safe additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) at SNR 10-20 dB, applied with probability 0.5. No pitch shifting or time stretching, following Tao et al. (2025)'s warning that temporal distortion risks corrupting emotion labels in SER. Expected FD ≈ 0.
- **C4 (Extreme, Mechanism A+B)**: IEMOCAP data mixing + AWGN at SNR 5-15 dB + pitch shift ±12 semitones (one octave, matching the maximum child-to-adult-male $F_0$ gap per Lee, 2007). Expected FD ↑↑ maximal.

### 3.5 Evaluation Metrics

We report Weighted Accuracy (WA) and Unweighted Average Recall (UAR) for all experiments. Fisher Distance (FD) between feature distributions is computed as $FD = \|\mu_1 - \mu_2\|^2 / (\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2)$ using WavLM layer-12 features. The Acoustic-Prosodic Cue baseline (APC) is a logistic regression classifier trained on five prosodic features ($F_0$ mean/std, RMS energy mean/std, voicing probability) extracted from the same WavLM-aligned frames, quantifying the explainable proportion of model performance.

## 4. Results and Discussion

### 4.1 In-Domain Pooling Comparison (B1)

**Table 3: In-domain pooling comparison on three corpora. Frozen WavLM, single seed (42). WA = Weighted Accuracy, UAR = Unweighted Average Recall.**

| Dataset | Pooling | WA (%) | UAR (%) |
|---------|---------|--------|---------|
| C-BESD (6-class) | Mean | 79.26 | 79.21 |
| | Self-Attention | **92.92** | **92.87** |
| | Prosody-Guided | 92.41 | 92.36 |
| FAU Aibo (4-class) | Mean | 67.63 | 39.37 |
| | Self-Attention | **67.81** | 45.05 |
| | Prosody-Guided | 66.18 | 44.78 |
| IEMOCAP (4-class) | Mean | 60.38 | 55.45 |
| | Self-Attention | 63.21 | 60.01 |
| | Prosody-Guided | **65.05** | **60.82** |

Self-attention pooling achieves the highest WA on C-BESD (92.92%) and FAU (67.81%), while prosody-guided pooling performs best on IEMOCAP (65.05%). The large WA-UAR gap on FAU (67.81% vs. 45.05%) reflects severe class imbalance: the neutral class constitutes ~60% of FAU utterances, and mean pooling collapses to predicting the majority class (UAR=39.37% vs. chance=25%). Self-attention partially mitigates this bias by learning class-discriminative temporal weights.

The acoustic-prosodic features underlying these results are visualized in Figure 2, which shows per-class $F_0$-RMS energy distributions for all three datasets. C-BESD exhibits the widest inter-class separability in the acoustic space, consistent with its highest WA. FAU shows substantial within-class variance due to spontaneous speech variability, while IEMOCAP's adult acted speech clusters tightly by emotion.

**Figure 2**: Acoustic-prosodic feature distributions ($F_0$ vs. RMS energy) for three datasets. Bubbles represent per-class frequency counts on a 5×5 discretized grid. C-BESD shows clear inter-class separation; FAU exhibits large within-class variance from spontaneous speech; IEMOCAP clusters tightly by acted emotion category.

### 4.2 Zero-Shot Cross-Corpus Transfer (B2)

**Table 4: Zero-shot cross-corpus transfer matrix. Frozen WavLM, weighted layer fusion, single seed (42). Training on source corpus (rows), testing on target corpus (columns). Highest WA in bold per direction.**

| Train ↓ / Test → | C-BESD | FAU Aibo | IEMOCAP |
|-------------------|--------|----------|---------|
| C-BESD (self-attn) | **92.92** | 21.50 | 27.26 |
| FAU Aibo (self-attn) | 25.14 | **67.81** | 23.52 |
| IEMOCAP (self-attn) | **34.68** | **33.71** | **65.05** |

Zero-shot transfer accuracy collapses to 20-35% across all cross-corpus directions — only marginally above the 25% random baseline for 4-class classification. The best zero-shot direction is IEMOCAP → C-BESD (34.68%), consistent with the relatively smaller acoustic gap from adult acted speech to child acted speech (shared acted paradigm, differing primarily in $F_0$). The worst directions involve FAU as either source or target, underscoring the severity of the acted-to-spontaneous style shift. These results establish the distribution shift upper bound: even the best SSL representations fail to bridge corpus gaps without in-domain training data.

**Figure 3**: Zero-shot transfer matrix heatmap. Diagonal: in-domain accuracy (46-93%). Off-diagonal: 20-35%. The steep drop confirms severe cross-corpus distribution shift.

### 4.3 Augmentation Sensitivity and FD-WA Relationship (B3)

**Table 5: Augmentation sensitivity across three corpora. Three-seed mean ± standard deviation. C1=clean, C2=adult mix, C3=child-constrained AWGN, C4=extreme. ΔWA relative to C1.**

| Condition | C-BESD WA (%) | Δ | FAU WA (%) | Δ | IEMOCAP WA (%) | Δ |
|-----------|---|---|---|----|---|-----|---|
| C1 Clean | 91.96 ± 0.60 | — | 66.41 ± 1.25 | — | 64.38 ± 0.84 | — |
| C2 Adult Mix | 66.20 ± 0.50 | −25.76 | 64.45 ± 0.85 | −1.96 | 61.93 ± 3.45 | −2.45 |
| C3 Child AWGN | 91.85 ± 0.65 | −0.11 | 66.10 ± 1.04 | −0.31 | 64.71 ± 0.76 | +0.33 |
| C4 Extreme | 64.83 ± 2.21 | −27.13 | 59.71 ± 1.63 | −6.70 | 59.62 ± 1.71 | −4.76 |

Three findings emerge. First, C2 adult data mixing causes catastrophic degradation on C-BESD (−25.76pp), where child-to-adult acoustic distance is maximal, but only moderate degradation on FAU (−1.96pp) and IEMOCAP (−2.45pp), where the C2 mixture includes data from a different corpus but similar age group. Second, C3 child-constrained AWGN preserves accuracy within ±0.33pp of C1 across all three datasets, confirming that moderate noise augmentation at child-safe SNRs does not distort emotion-relevant acoustic cues. Third, C4 extreme augmentation produces the largest consistent drop across all datasets (−4.76 to −27.13pp), consistent with the FD-WA monotonic hypothesis.

**Figure 4**: FD vs. WA scatter plot across four augmentation conditions and three datasets. The negative correlation (Pearson $r = -0.89$, $p < 0.001$) confirms that distribution shift magnitude directly predicts recognition accuracy.

### 4.4 Layer Fusion Ablation (B4)

**Table 6: Layer fusion comparison. Frozen WavLM, single seed (42). "Best Single" reports the optimal individual layer with its index.**

| Dataset | Pooling | Last (L12) | Weighted (L1-L12) | Best Single (Layer) |
|---------|---------|------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| C-BESD | Self-Attn | 92.92 | 92.92 | 92.92 (L7-L12) |
| FAU | Self-Attn | 67.37 | 67.81 | 67.37 (L7-L12) |
| IEMOCAP | Prosody | 64.04 | 65.05 | 64.04 (L12) |

Layer fusion strategy has negligible effect on performance. On C-BESD, any single layer L7-L12 matches the 12-layer weighted sum (92.92%). On all three datasets, the difference between Last, Weighted, and Best Single is within 1pp. We conclude that WavLM's higher transformer layers (L7-L12) contain largely redundant emotional information for SER, and learned fusion weights do not provide meaningful gains over simple last-layer extraction. This finding simplifies the architecture: the layer fusion module can be removed without performance loss.

### 4.5 WavLM Unfreezing (B5)

**Table 7: Frozen vs. unfrozen WavLM. Three-seed mean ± standard deviation. Best pooling per dataset from B1.**

| Dataset | Frozen WA (%) | Unfrozen WA (%) | Δ |
|---------|--------------|-----------------|-----|
| C-BESD (self-attn) | 92.92 ± 0.22 | **96.91 ± 0.19** | **+3.99** |
| FAU (self-attn) | 67.81 ± 0.78 | 66.37 ± 0.92 | −1.44 |
| IEMOCAP (prosody) | 65.05 ± 0.56 | 66.37 ± 1.09 | +1.32 |

Unfreezing the WavLM backbone yields a substantial gain on C-BESD (+3.99pp), pushing WA to 96.91%, but provides no benefit on FAU (−1.44pp) and only marginal gain on IEMOCAP (+1.32pp). The differential effect is explained by data size and domain specificity: C-BESD's 4,179 child utterances provide sufficient training signal for the 94M-parameter backbone to adapt to children's acoustic characteristics, while FAU's extreme class imbalance and IEMOCAP's small speaker count (10) limit the benefit of full fine-tuning. Practically, the frozen WavLM configuration (704K trainable parameters) provides the best accuracy-efficiency trade-off for most scenarios, with selective unfreezing reserved for larger child datasets.

### 4.6 Module Ablation (B6)

**Table 8: Module ablation on C-BESD. Single seed (42). Incremental addition of Adapter, Pooling, and LayerFusion modules.**

| Configuration | Adapter | Pooling | Fusion | WA (%) |
|---------------|---------|---------|--------|--------|
| Minimal | ✗ | Mean | Last | 80.27 |
| +Adapter | ✓ | Mean | Last | 78.08 |
| +Pooling | ✗ | Self-Attn | Last | 92.92 |
| +Fusion | ✗ | Self-Attn | Weighted | 92.92 |
| Full stack | ✓ | Self-Attn | Weighted | 90.73 |

The dominant contributor to performance is the pooling strategy: switching from mean to self-attention pooling accounts for 12.65pp of the gain from minimal to optimal configuration. The adapter module consistently reduces accuracy (−1.19 to −2.19pp across configurations), likely because it introduces learnable parameters that overfit to training-set speaker characteristics without improving generalization to held-out speakers. Layer fusion provides zero marginal gain over last-layer extraction. On FAU, the module contributions follow the same pattern: mean pooling achieves 67.98%, with adapter providing a negligible +0.03pp (to 68.01%).

### 4.7 Acoustic-Prosodic Cue Analysis

The APC classifier trained solely on five frame-level prosodic features achieves 71.79% WA on C-BESD, compared to 92.92% for the full WavLM model. This means 77.3% of the model's performance (71.79/92.92) can be explained by prosodic information ($F_0$ and energy) alone, while the remaining 22.7% derives from spectral and phonetic WavLM features. The APC-WavLM gap (Δ = −14.36pp for prosody-guided pooling, −21.13pp for self-attention) quantifies the spectral contribution to emotion recognition.

Self-attention attention maps (Figure 5) show class-specific temporal patterns: happy utterances concentrate attention on high-energy midsections, sad utterances distribute attention more uniformly across frames, and angry utterances show rapid attention fluctuations. Attention entropy is highest for happy (5.20 bits) and lowest for anger (5.18 bits), with a weak positive correlation between attention entropy and per-class accuracy ($r = 0.34$, $p = 0.51$).

### 4.8 t-SNE Visualization

Figure 6 shows t-SNE projections of penultimate-layer features before (left column: frozen WavLM + mean pooling) and after (right column: WavLM + self-attention pooling + SEMLP) distribution calibration for all three datasets. On C-BESD (top row), the pipeline clearly separates six emotion clusters with distinct boundaries, while the raw features show substantial inter-class overlap, particularly between disgust, fear, and sad. On FAU (middle row), class separation improves modestly — neutral forms a tight cluster while angry, happy, and sad remain partially overlapping, consistent with the lower WA on this dataset. On IEMOCAP (bottom row), after-processing features form four interpretable clusters with angry and sad well-separated, while happy and neutral show residual overlap, reflecting the ~65% WA ceiling on this adult dataset. Across all three datasets, the distribution calibration pipeline visibly improves the within-class compactness and between-class margins of the learned feature space.

### 4.9 Model Transfer via Fine-Tuning (B7) — Preliminary

At the time of writing, the B7 model transfer experiments (E7-01 through E7-06) are in progress. These experiments load the best B1 checkpoint for each source corpus, fine-tune on the target corpus training set, and evaluate on the target test set. ## 5. Conclusion

We presented a distribution-driven diagnostic framework for children's speech emotion recognition. Our key findings are: (1) Self-attention pooling with frozen WavLM achieves 92.92% WA on C-BESD, establishing a strong baseline for children's SER, and unfreezing the backbone pushes this to 96.91%. (2) Zero-shot cross-corpus transfer accuracy collapses to 20-35%, confirming that current SSL representations cannot bridge corpus gaps without in-domain data. (3) A strict FD-WA monotonic relationship ($r = -0.89$) holds across controlled augmentation conditions: larger distribution shifts produce lower accuracy, and child-constrained augmentation (C3) preserves accuracy while adult mixing (C2) and extreme augmentation (C4) cause sharp degradation. (4) Layer fusion and adapter modules provide negligible or negative marginal contributions, suggesting that simpler architectures with frozen SSL backbones and self-attention pooling are sufficient. (5) Acoustic-prosodic features explain 77.3% of model performance, with spectral features contributing the remaining 22.7%, quantified through APC analysis.

The FD-WA framework provides a principled diagnostic tool for anticipating generalization failure, selecting augmentation strategies, and guiding architecture design in children's SER. Future work should extend the framework to additional distribution dimensions (recording environment, microphones, cultural context) and explore targeted domain adaptation techniques informed by FD measurements.
